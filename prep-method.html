How to follow the plan

The rule

Do not confuse completion with readiness.

The dashboard is designed to make you interview-ready, not simply to help you finish a syllabus. Every week should end with evidence that you can use the skill without notes.

Weekdays: 45–75 min · Saturday/Sunday: 5–7h

Daily loop

1. Learn

Use the linked primary resource. Take short notes only on concepts you cannot explain from first principles.

2. Practice

Solve the assigned problems or complete the design/code exercise. Do not immediately look at a solution.

3. Explain

Say the approach aloud. For system design, explain requirements, scale, architecture, failures and trade-offs.

4. Review

Record mistakes and one reusable insight. Anything you cannot explain tomorrow stays in the revision queue.

Weekly gate — pass before considering the week complete

DSA gate

  • At least 80% of assigned problems solved without looking at the solution.
  • You can name the pattern before coding.
  • You can explain time/space complexity and edge cases.

System-design gate

  • One 45–60 minute design completed without a template.
  • Requirements, scale, APIs, storage, caching, failures and trade-offs covered.
  • You can defend why you chose each major component.

Backend-depth gate

  • You can explain the week's backend concept from first principles.
  • You can describe at least two failure modes and mitigations.
  • You can relate the topic to your real Java/Spring/TiDB/ES/Kafka/Redis experience.

Communication gate

  • You can explain the topic aloud clearly in 5–10 minutes.
  • You avoid jumping directly to technology names before clarifying constraints.

Resource hierarchy

DSA: NeetCode roadmap for pattern sequencing → LeetCode for actual timed practice.

System design: Hello Interview for structured concepts/frameworks → System Design Primer for breadth/reference → your own mock designs for retention.

Backend: official Java/Spring/MySQL/TiDB/Redis/Kafka/Elasticsearch docs for correctness → interview-oriented material only for explanation and practice.

Company prep: official company interview guidance first, then carefully selected recent candidate reports. Do not overfit to alleged “leaked questions.”

Interview calibration

Weeks 1–8: core engineering dominates. Weeks 9–12: add mocks and increase company-specific calibration. Weeks 13–16: target-company loops, timing, project defense and behavioral depth become the center of the plan.

Most important rule

Protect the weekends.

Because you work full-time, your Saturday/Sunday deep-work blocks are the highest-leverage part of the program. If a weekday session is missed, recover it on the weekend rather than doubling your workload after office.

Never sacrifice sleep repeatedly to “finish the syllabus.” Interview performance needs recall, reasoning speed and calm communication.
